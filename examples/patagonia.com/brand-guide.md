## Tone of voice

Action-led and collectively minded, pairing vivid outdoor language with direct environmental commitments and detailed, evidence-based advocacy.

### Business as Activism

Treats environmental action as a core business responsibility, not a peripheral cause.

- **Do:** Name the action Patagonia takes and the environmental interests it serves.
- **Don't:** Substitute vague statements about caring for concrete commitments.

> "Action Is Our Business" ([source](https://www.patagonia.com/activism))
> "We’re Taxing Ourselves" ([source](https://www.patagonia.com/activism))

### Collective and Mobilizing

Uses shared responsibility and practical invitations to move people toward participation.

- **Do:** Use we and our to frame shared stakes, then offer a specific way to participate.
- **Don't:** Leave readers with an abstract problem and no next step.

> "Together, we can change that." ([source](https://www.patagonia.com/))
> "But as threats to our home planet grow, so does the need for collective action." ([source](https://www.patagonia.com/activism))

### Outdoor-Specific and Evocative

Grounds product language in recognizable outdoor experiences rather than generic lifestyle aspiration.

- **Do:** Use concrete terrain, activities and moments to describe what gear enables.
- **Don't:** Rely on luxury language or empty claims about adventure.

> "Our Winter ’26 New Arrivals are made for the moments you chase—first chair, fresh trails and far-flung summits." ([source](https://www.patagonia.com/))
> "It’s gear that meets you in the moment and helps keep you there." ([source](https://www.patagonia.com/))

### Firm, Evidence-Led Advocacy

States a clear position and supports it with policy detail, economic stakes and quantified evidence.

- **Do:** Pair policy positions with named rules, specific consequences and relevant numbers.
- **Don't:** Replace reasoned opposition with insults or unsupported certainty.

> "We write in strong opposition to the U.S. Department of Agriculture's (USDA) proposal to rescind the 2001 Roadless Rule." ([source](https://www.patagonia.com/us/press/86586))
> "A February 2026 national poll found 76 percent of likely voters support keeping the Roadless Rule intact.[3]" ([source](https://www.patagonia.com/us/press/86586))

**Tone spectrum** (0 to 100)

- Formal 35 Casual
- Serious 8 Playful
- Technical 30 Plain
- Understated 72 Bold

**Style rules**

- Use Title Case for most headings; reserve all caps for a short campaign statement like the home-page slogan.
- Aim for sentences around 19 words, allowing longer, information-dense sentences in policy advocacy.
- Favor first-person plural for commitments and shared responsibility; use second person selectively for gear and participation.
- Use declarative sentences and direct imperatives, without exclamation marks or rhetorical questions.
- Use contractions in consumer and activism copy; adopt more formal phrasing in official policy correspondence.
- Preserve precise figures, dates and policy names; expand technical acronyms on first mention in advocacy copy.

**Words we use:** take action, environmental, community, communities, our business, outdoor, protect, wild places, stories, Roadless Rule

**Words we avoid:** luxury, exclusive, effortless, planet-saving miracle, eco-friendly vibes, revolutionary, buy now!!!

**Measured:** 18.8 words per sentence, reading ease 26 (Very dense (specialist)), headings in Title Case, "you" 0.8 per 100 words vs "we" 3.4.

## Messaging

- **Outdoor recreationists:** Gear for skiing, trails and summits, alongside protection of the wild places their recreation depends on.
- **Environmental advocates and volunteers:** Ways to connect with environmental groups through events, petitions, skilled volunteering and donations.
- **Environmental nonprofits:** Funding, community participation and support for protecting air, land and water.
- **Policymakers and outdoor-economy communities:** Evidence connecting conservation with livelihoods, public support and long-term economic stability.

### Gear for the Moment, Built for a Lifetime

Patagonia presents outdoor gear as made for specific experiences and built for lasting use.

> "MADE FOR THE MOMENT. BUILT FOR A LIFETIME." ([source](https://www.patagonia.com/))
> "It’s gear that meets you in the moment and helps keep you there." ([source](https://www.patagonia.com/))

### Business Powers Environmental Action

Patagonia puts business resources and self-imposed funding behind grassroots environmental protection.

> "Since 1985, we’ve used our business, voice and resources to build power in the grassroots environmental movement." ([source](https://www.patagonia.com/activism))
> "Patagonia’s self-imposed Earth tax, 1% for the Planet, provides support to environmental nonprofits working to defend our air, land and water around the globe." ([source](https://www.patagonia.com/activism))

### Collective Action Starts with Participation

Patagonia invites individuals to join environmental groups and take practical action on urgent issues.

> "We’re connecting individuals with Patagonia grantees, to take action on the most pressing issues facing the world today." ([source](https://www.patagonia.com/activism))
> "11.2M environmental voters skipped the last election. Together, we can change that." ([source](https://www.patagonia.com/))

### Healthy Wild Places Sustain Livelihoods

Protecting wild places supports Patagonia’s business, outdoor recreation and the communities that depend on them.

> "The future of our business, the outdoor recreation economy and our planet relies on the health of wild places, including inventoried roadless areas." ([source](https://www.patagonia.com/us/press/86586))
> "That certainty has helped businesses like ours invest in gateway communities and build lasting partnerships." ([source](https://www.patagonia.com/us/press/86586))
