## Tone of voice

Duolingo pairs playful encouragement with learning science. It speaks directly to learners, makes progress feel achievable, and explains practical benefits without losing its sense of fun.

### Playful with a purpose

Fun is presented as a way to sustain meaningful learning, not as a distraction from it.

- **Do:** Connect playful features or short learning breaks to progress, skills, or habit-building.
- **Don't:** Use jokes or game language without explaining the learning benefit.

> "serious learning, made fun for all" ([source](https://www.duolingo.com/business))
> "Short, varied lessons make it fun to take a learning break" ([source](https://www.duolingo.com/business))

### Science made approachable

Research and expertise provide credibility, while explanations remain focused on understandable learner outcomes.

- **Do:** Name the scientific foundation and immediately explain what it helps learners do.
- **Don't:** Bury the benefit under academic terminology or imply unsupported results.

> "Learning with Duolingo is fun, and research shows that it works!" ([source](https://www.duolingo.com/))
> "Combining the best of AI and language science, lessons are tailored to help you learn at just the right level and pace." ([source](https://www.duolingo.com/))

### Progress-positive

The voice encourages steady progress, confidence, and healthy habits rather than pressure or perfection.

- **Do:** Emphasize achievable steps, encouragement, and continued progress.
- **Don't:** Shame learners for mistakes or frame learning as a test of personal worth.

> "Duolingo focuses on making progress, which builds confidence and healthy habits" ([source](https://www.duolingo.com/business))
> "Employees can follow each other and send congrats for making progress" ([source](https://www.duolingo.com/business))

### Curious and learner-led

Questions invite learners into a conversation, with experts positioned as helpful guides.

- **Do:** Use real learner questions as headings and address the reader directly.
- **Don't:** Talk down to beginners or assume readers already know specialist terms.

> "From your nerdiest emails about learning to the science of how your brain works, you've got questions—and Duolingo experts have answers." ([source](https://blog.duolingo.com))
> "11 chess terms every beginner needs to know" ([source](https://blog.duolingo.com))

**Tone spectrum** (0 to 100)

- Formal 73 Casual
- Serious 66 Playful
- Technical 73 Plain
- Understated 64 Bold

**Style rules**

- Use sentence-case editorial headings and lowercase promotional headings; avoid title case.
- Keep explanatory sentences near 19 words on average, with short benefit-led headings to break up longer copy.
- Address readers as 'you' and 'your'; use 'we' when explaining how Duolingo supports learning.
- Use questions frequently in advice and FAQs, rather than making every promotional statement a question.
- Use contractions for a conversational feel and reserve exclamation marks for occasional enthusiastic emphasis.
- Keep technical terms tied to plain-language benefits; use numerals for quantities and uppercase for prominent CTA labels.

**Words we use:** fun, effective, learning, progress, bite-sized, personalized, learners, research, science, everyone, get started

**Words we avoid:** effortless mastery, guaranteed fluency, punishment, failure, elite-only, pedagogical optimization, enterprise synergies

**Measured:** 18.8 words per sentence, reading ease 50 (Plain (high-school level)), headings in Sentence case, "you" 2.1 per 100 words vs "we" 1.4.
